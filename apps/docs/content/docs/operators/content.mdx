Public pages are content entries edited under **Content** in the console. Nothing on the marketing site needs a deploy to change.

## Kinds of content

| Kind | Slug | What it renders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page | `home` | The home page: hero and terminal sample, stats, features, steps, request-flow diagram, use cases, FAQ, closing call to action. |
| Pricing page | `pricing` | Plan cards and FAQ. Plan *limits* in the comparison table come live from the plan settings, so they never disagree with what is enforced. |
| Page | anything, e.g. `terms`, `legal/privacy` | Markdown at `/p/<slug>`. Optionally listed in the footer under Product, Company or Legal. Raw HTML is not rendered. |

The landing and pricing editors are forms; pages are a markdown editor with a preview.

## Draft, publish, roll back

- **Save** keeps a working copy. Visitors keep seeing the published version.
- **Publish** makes the working copy live. The website picks it up within a minute.
- **Unpublish** takes your version offline. Entries that ship with a built-in default (home, pricing, terms and privacy) fall back to it; any other page answers 404.
- Every publish keeps a **revision** (with an optional note). *Restore* copies an old revision into the working copy; publish to make it live.
- Saves are last-write-wins. If two admins edit the same entry at once, agree who publishes.

If the API is unreachable, the website renders the built-in defaults rather than an error.

## Who can do what

`content.read` to view, `content.update` to save drafts and restore revisions, `content.publish` to publish and unpublish, `content.delete` to delete an entry. Deleting an entry that has a built-in default brings the default back.

## Starting from a built-in page

Until you customise them, home, pricing, terms and privacy show built-in default content. The Content screen lists these under *Built-in pages using default content*; click one to copy the default into an editable entry. Have your legal team review the default terms and privacy pages before launch: they are a starting point, not legal advice.
