Public pages are content entries edited under **Website content** in the console. Nothing on the marketing site needs a deploy to change. Blog posts have their own screen: see [Blog](/operators/blog). Images come from the [media library](/operators/media).

Every public page (pricing, pages, blog, support, the 404 page) shares the landing page's dark *void* look: the starfield behind the navbar, glass cards and the gradient buttons. Content can't change the colours or fonts, so new pages match automatically.

## Kinds of content

| Kind | Slug | What it renders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page | `home` | The home page: hero (with an animated wormhole and the terminal sample), stats, the journey of a request (the stops a request passes on its way to your app, played one by one), features, steps, an optional sequence diagram, use cases, FAQ, closing call to action, and three sections you fill in when you have something to show (hidden while empty): **logos** of customers under the hero, a **showcase** (a YouTube or Vimeo link or a video file, and/or screenshots with captions) and **testimonials** (quote, name, role, optional photo). Images come from the media library. Landing content saved before a section existed gets it when opened in the editor; the journey shows the built-in one until you publish. |
| Pricing page | `pricing` | Plan cards and FAQ. Plan *limits* in the comparison table come live from the plan settings, so they never disagree with what is enforced. Each plan can have a yearly price and period, and the page has a badge for the yearly switch ("2 months free"): see [Plans and billing](/operators/plans-and-billing). |
| Page | anything else, e.g. `about`, `contact`, `legal/privacy` | A page at `/<slug>`, written in the rich text editor. |
| Footer menu | `footer` | The footer of every page: columns of links, in order. |

The landing and pricing editors are forms. Pages and the footer have their own editors, below.

## Pages

**New page** asks for a title and a slug; the page is served at `/<slug>` (for example `/about`). Slugs the website already uses are refused: `login`, `register`, `pricing`, `docs`, `support`, `dashboard`, `organizations`, `api`, `p`, `blog`, `changelog`, `media`, `newsletter`, `preview`, the language codes and the others in `RESERVED_PAGE_SLUGS` (packages/content). The website's own pages always win over a page with the same path, so a page can never hide one.

The editor works like a word processor: headings, bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, links, bulleted and numbered lists, quotes, code blocks, tables and dividers, with undo. **Markdown** switches to the page's source, for pasting or bulk edits.

Pages are stored as markdown, and the website shows them with raw HTML switched off. So a page can't run scripts or embed other sites, and colours and fonts follow the website's design rather than the page. Links can go to `https://`, `mailto:`, a path on the site (`/pricing`) or a section (`#faq`).

Pages that used to live at `/p/<slug>` redirect permanently to `/<slug>`. A path with no page answers a real 404, with a page that shows the address and links to the home page, blog, docs and support.

Each entry has a search title and description for search results and link previews. The [SEO](/operators/seo) screen shows how every page does in search, can override those per address, and lists the missing addresses visitors hit, so you can redirect them.

### Contact form

**+ Contact form** places a contact form in the page (one per page). Visitors enter their name, email, an optional subject and a message. Each message:

- is listed under **Messages** in the console (unread first, then read; archived ones under *Archived*), where it can be marked unread, archived or deleted, and replied to by email;
- is emailed to the **Support email** setting, with the visitor's address as Reply-To.

Spam protection:

- A hidden field that people never fill. Filled ones are dropped without telling the sender.
- A limit of 5 messages per 10 minutes per address.
- Optionally, a Cloudflare Turnstile check, which most visitors pass without clicking anything. To turn it on:
  1. Create a widget in the Cloudflare dashboard, for the website's domain.
  2. Put its secret key in the API's `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` (see [Deployment](/operators/deployment)).
  3. Put its site key in the **Captcha site key** setting (Support).

  The form then asks for the check and the API refuses messages without a valid one. If Cloudflare can't be reached, the API answers 503 rather than letting messages through unchecked. The **Contact form** setting under Support switches the form off; pages then show the support email address instead.

In the markdown source the form is a line holding only `{{contact-form}}`.

## Footer menu

Until you publish a footer menu, the footer is built in:

- **Product**: Features, Pricing, Docs, and Changelog once an entry is published (see [Changelog](/operators/changelog)).
- **Company**: About and Contact, Blog (once a post is live), Support, and Status, GitHub and X when those settings are set.
- **Legal**: Terms of Service, Privacy Policy.

Published pages marked for the footer join their column. When the [cookie banner](/operators/analytics) is in use, the last column also gets **Cookie settings**.

*Customize Footer menu* on the Website content screen creates the footer menu as a copy of that footer, so nothing disappears when you switch. Then:

- **Columns**: up to 6, each with a title. An empty column is not shown.
- **Links**: up to 12 per column. A link goes to **a page** (picked from the list) or **a URL** (`https://…`, `mailto:…` or a path such as `/pricing` or `/#features`), and can open in a new tab.
- **Order**: drag a column or a link by its ⠿ handle, or focus the handle and use Space and the arrow keys. *Column* moves a link to another column.

A link to a page shows only while that page is published, so unpublishing or deleting a page takes it out of the footer at the same time.

## Draft, publish, roll back

- **Save draft** keeps a working copy. Visitors keep seeing the published version.
- **Preview ↗** opens the working copy on the website, with the real layout, under a *Draft preview* banner. Unsaved changes are saved first (the button then reads *Save & preview ↗*). The preview address works for 48 hours for anyone who has it, without signing in, so you can send it to someone for review. Search engines are told not to index it, and it is never cached. The footer has no preview: publish it to see it.
- **Publish** makes the working copy live. The website shows it on the next request when the website refresh is set up (`WEB_REVALIDATE_URL` and `WEB_REVALIDATE_SECRET` on the API, `REVALIDATE_SECRET` on the website; see [Deployment](/operators/deployment)); otherwise within a minute. Saving settings refreshes the site the same way.
- **Unpublish** takes your version offline. Entries that ship with a built-in default (home, pricing, about, contact, terms and privacy) fall back to it; any other page answers 404; the footer goes back to the built-in one.
- Every publish keeps a **revision** (with an optional note). *Restore* copies an old revision into the working copy; publish to make it live.
- If another admin saved the same entry after you opened it, your save is refused with a message to reload; their changes are never overwritten silently. Reload, then make your change again.

If the API is unreachable, the website renders the built-in defaults rather than an error.

## Who can do what

`content.read` to view and preview, `content.update` to save drafts and restore revisions, `content.publish` to publish and unpublish, `content.delete` to delete an entry. Deleting an entry that has a built-in default brings the default back. `contact.read` lists contact form messages; `contact.update` marks them read, archives and deletes them.

## Starting from built-in content

Until you customise them, home, pricing, about, contact, terms, privacy and the footer show built-in content. The built-in Contact page holds the contact form; About describes the product in general terms and is meant to be rewritten. The Website content screen lists these under *Built-in content you can customize*; click one to copy it into an editable entry. Have your legal team review the default terms and privacy pages before launch: they are a starting point, not legal advice. Built-in pages are in the sitemap and on the SEO screen (marked *built-in*) like your own.
