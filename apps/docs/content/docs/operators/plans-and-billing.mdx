Everything on this page is on **Plans & pricing** in the admin console.

## Launch for free, charge later

**Billing mode** has two values:

- **Free** (the default): nobody is asked to pay. Upgrade buttons are hidden, checkout is refused, the pricing page shows a single free plan with your *free mode message*, and Stripe does not need to be configured at all.
- **Paid**: Pro and Enterprise can be bought through Stripe checkout.

Switching back and forth never touches existing subscriptions: an account that pays keeps paying and keeps its plan.

**Plan for accounts that don't pay** (`billing.defaultPlan`) decides what everyone without a paid subscription gets. During a launch you can set it to PRO to be generous, and edit each plan's limits below. When you later switch to paid mode, set it back to FREE: accounts without a subscription drop to the FREE limits within a minute.

## Where a limit comes from

Each account's effective limits are worked out in three layers, last one wins:

1. **Built-in plan limits** for FREE, PRO and ENTERPRISE, shipped with the code. Which plan an account is on: its paying subscription's plan, otherwise the default plan above.
2. **Plan overrides** (`plans.overrides` under **Settings → Plan limits**): change a limit for every account on a plan, for example raising FREE to 2 agents.
3. **Account overrides** (the *Limit overrides* tab on an account): change a limit for one account only.

`inspectorRequests` is how many recent requests the [request inspector](/dashboard/request-inspector) keeps per tunnel (0 turns it off for that plan or account). The inspector as a whole can be switched off with `features.requestInspector`. `inboxRequests` is how many requests a tunnel's [webhook inbox](/dashboard/webhook-inbox) holds while its agent is offline (0 = no inbox; `features.webhookInbox` turns it off for everyone). `accessRules` decides who can create [tunnel access rules](/dashboard/tunnel-access) (password, IP allowlist, share links); rules that exist keep working after a downgrade.
`maxCustomDomains` is how many [custom domains](/dashboard/custom-domains) an account can add (0 hides the form; domains that exist keep working after a downgrade), with `features.customDomains` as the platform-wide switch. `maxAlertRules` caps [alert rules](/dashboard/alerts) per account (`features.alerts` stops all alert evaluation). `maxMockApis` and `maxMockEndpoints` cap [mock APIs](/dashboard/mock-apis) per account and endpoints per mock (FREE 2 and 25, PRO 25 and 250, ENTERPRISE 250 and 1,000; `features.mockApis` off makes the hub stop answering from mocks). `maxApiCollections`, `maxApiCollectionRequests` and `apiClientSendsPerMinute` limit the [API client](/dashboard/api-client): collections per account (0 turns it off for the account), requests per collection, and requests the platform sends for the account each minute, collection runs included (FREE 3, 50 and 30; PRO 50, 500 and 300; ENTERPRISE 500, 2,000 and 1,200; `features.apiClient` off refuses sending and runs everywhere, keeping saved collections). `maxLoadTestVus`, `maxLoadTestSeconds`, `maxLoadTestRps` and `loadTestsPerDay` bound [load tests](/dashboard/performance#load-tests) (FREE 10 users, 60 s, 50/s, 5 a day; PRO 100, 300 s, 500/s, 100; ENTERPRISE 500, 900 s, 2,000/s, 1,000; 0 users turns them off for the account), and `maxMonitors` and `minMonitorIntervalMinutes` bound [monitors](/dashboard/performance#monitors) (FREE 1 every 15 min; PRO 20 and ENTERPRISE 200, every minute); `features.performance` off refuses new load tests, stops running ones and pauses monitors. `maxTrafficRules` caps [traffic rules](/dashboard/traffic-rules) per tunnel (0 hides them; `features.trafficRules` off makes the hub ignore every rule at once, for example during an incident).

In both override editors an empty field means "inherit", and *Unlimited* removes the cap. **System → Effective limits** shows the result of layers 1 and 2; the account page shows all three.

The API, the hub and the public pricing table all read the same effective values, so what you see on the pricing page is what is enforced.

## Checkout

Users upgrade from **Billing** in their workspace (paid mode only). The browser sends the plan name only; the API maps it to the plan's Stripe price and only accepts return URLs on your own site.

**Prices.** Create a product with a monthly price for each paid plan in the Stripe dashboard and paste the price IDs (`price_…`) into **Plans & pricing → Payments**. *Save and check* looks the price up in Stripe and shows its amount, interval and whether it is active, so a typo shows up immediately. The `STRIPE_PRO_PRICE_ID` / `STRIPE_ENTERPRISE_PRICE_ID` environment variables still work as a fallback. To change a price, create a new price in Stripe and paste its ID; existing subscribers stay on the price they bought until you migrate them in Stripe. The price *shown* on the pricing page is text you edit under **Website content → Pricing page**; keep the two the same.

If paid mode is on but something is missing (Stripe keys, a price, an archived price), the screen lists exactly what.

- `billing.checkoutEnabled` off hides *Upgrade* in the dashboard and refuses new checkouts. Existing subscriptions are untouched. Use it while prices are being changed.
- `billing.trialDays` sets the free trial for an account's **first** subscription. Later subscriptions start paid. `0` disables trials.
- An account whose subscription is past due or unpaid cannot start a second checkout; it has to fix payment in the Stripe customer portal first.

Prices, currencies, taxes, coupons and refunds are managed in the Stripe dashboard.

## When payment fails

Stripe retries according to your Stripe settings. The account moves to *past due* (it keeps working) and the owner is emailed. If Stripe finally cancels the subscription, the account returns to the FREE plan's limits. Past-due accounts are listed on the console's **Billing** screen.
