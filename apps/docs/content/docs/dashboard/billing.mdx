Every account, personal or organization, has its own plan: **FREE**, **PRO** or **ENTERPRISE**. Plans are flat-rate, billed monthly through Stripe. Nothing is metered, so there is no charge per request. What each plan allows is on [Plans and limits](/reference/plans-and-limits).

During early access VhyxVoid may run **free for everyone**: then nothing can be bought, the **Upgrade** button is hidden, and the plan card shows the plan every account currently gets (it can be more than FREE) with a short note instead.

The **Billing** link is in the sidebar for **owners**, of an organization or of your personal workspace, so a personal workspace can be upgraded too. Other members can open the page by its address and see the plan, but the invoice history is for owners only ("Invoice history is only visible to organization owners.").

## The plan card

**Current plan** shows a badge for the plan and, while you have a paid subscription, a second one for its status, such as `TRIALING`, `ACTIVE`, `PAST_DUE` or `CANCELED`. For a paid plan it also shows **Next billing date** (or **Cancels on**, if the subscription is set to end) and **Trial ends** if you are on a trial.

## Upgrade

Without a paid subscription the card has an **Upgrade** button (unless VhyxVoid is in free mode, above). If upgrades are paused for a while, it says "Upgrades are temporarily unavailable." instead.

1. Choose **Upgrade** and pick **Pro** or **Enterprise**. The dialog shows each plan's monthly price and, when there is one, the length of the free trial a new subscription starts with (only an account's first subscription gets a trial).
2. Choose **Continue to checkout**. You are sent to Stripe's payment page; VhyxVoid never sees your card details.
3. After paying you come back to **Billing** with a banner: "Plan upgraded successfully. Welcome to your new plan!"

The plan applies to the account you upgraded from, not to your other accounts.

Owners and admins also get an in-app notice and an email when the account has used 80% and 100% of the month's included requests, and a reminder before a trial ends; see [Alerts](/dashboard/alerts#usage-and-trial-notices).

## Manage or cancel

With a paid plan, **Manage subscription** opens Stripe's customer portal, where you update the payment method, see invoices, and cancel or resume. When a subscription is set to cancel, the card shows a warning: "Your subscription will cancel at end of the current period."

When the paid period ends, the account goes back to the FREE plan's limits and keeps working: your tunnels, keys and members stay. If you are over a FREE limit (for example more agents than FREE allows), the extra ones are refused until you remove them or upgrade again.

<Callout type="info" title="Changed 2026-10-05">
  Before this release an account whose subscription ended was set to `CANCELED`, which stopped its tunnels and API keys entirely. Accounts left in that state are moved back to the FREE plan automatically.
</Callout>

## Invoices

Owners see **Invoice history**. Each row has the billing period (or "One-time payment"), a status (`PAID`, `OPEN`, `DRAFT`, `UNCOLLECTIBLE` or `VOID`), the date it was paid, and the amount. A **PDF** button downloads the invoice, and an unpaid invoice has a **Pay now** button.

## When a payment fails

When Stripe reports a failed payment:

1. The subscription and the account move to `PAST_DUE`, and the owner is emailed. The plan card turns red with "Payment failed. Please update your payment method to avoid service interruption." and an **Update payment** button that opens the Stripe portal.
2. Your plan's limits stay in force for a **seven day grace period**.
3. If it isn't resolved within that time, the account is `SUSPENDED` (a check runs hourly) and gets the FREE plan's limits.

<Callout type="info" title="Fixed in source, not yet deployed">
  Until a fix dated 2026-09-22 is deployed, step 2's seven-day deadline is never actually recorded, so step 3's scheduled check finds nothing to suspend and a `PAST_DUE` account stays `PAST_DUE` indefinitely. This is being corrected server-side; nothing to do on your end. Read from the code, not tested against a real overdue account.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Fixed 2026-09-22, not yet deployed: the grace period now keeps agents connected too">
  Until this ships, the hub only accepts an agent's connection when the account's status is `ACTIVE`, so `PAST_DUE` is refused exactly like `SUSPENDED` — the paragraph below describes the behavior once it's live, not today's. Read from the code, not tested against a real overdue account.
</Callout>

The hub accepts an agent's connection while the account is `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE` — the seven-day grace period keeps your tunnels running, not just your plan's limits. Once an account reaches `SUSPENDED` (or any other non-connectable status), a new connection is refused with `Account is not active`, and the hub also checks every **already-connected** agent's account roughly once a minute: an agent that was connected before the suspension gets disconnected within about a minute, with the same `Account is not active` reason, rather than continuing to run until it happens to reconnect on its own.

**While the account is `PAST_DUE`**, paying takes it back to `ACTIVE` automatically. **Once it reaches `SUSPENDED`**, paying alone no longer does: reactivating a suspended account is a deliberate step, not something a Stripe event does on its own (fixed 2026-09-22, not yet deployed — the account status doesn't currently record *why* it was suspended, so nothing can safely tell "suspended for non-payment" apart from any other kind of suspension). If your account is `SUSPENDED`, updating your payment method is still worth doing, but it won't reactivate the account by itself. You can see the account's status in the **My organizations** table on the [dashboard](/dashboard/account-and-organizations).
